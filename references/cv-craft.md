# CV Craft — writing rules (UK-first)

You are building a UK CV. Say CV, never résumé. Use British spelling throughout (organised, analysed, programme, optimise). Two pages A4 maximum. Everything below is a rule to apply, not a suggestion to consider.

Your levers are selection, reframing, reordering, and emphasis of what the candidate did. If a number is unknown, use a range or a scope proxy (see Quantifying below) — or drop the claim.

## Bullet anatomy

Every experience bullet is an achievement, not a duty. Test: could the worst performer in that job have written the same line? If yes, rewrite it.

Anatomy: **strong verb + what you did + how (method/tool) + quantified outcome.**

Use Google's XYZ formula as the default template: **"Accomplished X, as measured by Y, by doing Z."** In practice the natural word order is often verb-first: "Cut report turnaround from 5 days to 1 (Y) by automating data cleaning in Python (Z)."

CAR (Context–Action–Result) and STAR (Situation–Task–Action–Result) are the same DNA. On the page, compress to Action + Result; context lives in the role line (title, employer, dates). Keep the full STAR story for interviews, not the CV.

Rules:
- Start with a past-tense action verb (present tense only for the current role). Never "Responsible for", "Duties included", "Involved in", "Helped with", "Worked on".
- No first-person pronouns anywhere.
- One idea per bullet. One line preferred, two lines maximum.
- 3–6 bullets for recent/relevant roles; 1–2 for old or off-target roles.
- Lead each bullet with the most impressive element — usually the result.
- Vary verbs; never start two adjacent bullets with the same one.
- Name the tool/method when the target role cares about it ("in Python/pandas", "using Salesforce", "via A/B testing") — this is where job-ad keywords land naturally.

Verb bank by flavour: built, designed, automated, launched (delivery); led, coordinated, trained, mentored (people); analysed, modelled, forecast, evaluated (analysis); cut, reduced, saved, streamlined (efficiency); grew, increased, won, secured (growth); negotiated, presented, persuaded, resolved (influence).

Before/after:
- Weak: "Responsible for weekly sales reporting."
- Strong: "Automated weekly sales reporting in Excel/VBA, saving the team ~6 hours per week and eliminating manual copy errors."
- Weak: "Helped with customer queries."
- Strong: "Resolved 40–60 customer queries daily, maintaining a 95% first-contact resolution rate."
- Weak: "Involved in a project to improve onboarding."
- Strong: "Redesigned client onboarding checklist with 3 departments, cutting average setup time from 10 days to 4."

## Section design and order

Standard sections for an experienced hire, in order:
1. **Header** — name (large, top; never the words "Curriculum Vitae"), then phone, professional email, LinkedIn URL, city + postcode. Nothing else.
2. **Professional summary** (see below).
3. **Key skills** — optional but recommended; short clustered block (see Skills section).
4. **Experience** — reverse-chronological.
5. **Education & qualifications** — reverse-chronological.
6. **Additional** (only if it earns its space) — certifications, languages with level, volunteering, publications if relevant, interests only if distinctive or role-relevant.

Ordering logic — the reader must hit the strongest evidence for THIS role in the top third of page one:
- Experienced candidate: experience before education.
- Student/recent graduate: education before experience; include relevant modules, projects, dissertation.
- Career changer: summary + skills block carry the targeting load before the (still dated, still chronological) history — see career-narrative.md.
- Fresh qualification driving the change (e.g. a data-science MSc for an analyst role): move education up, even for an experienced candidate.

Exclude always: photo, date of birth, marital status, nationality, NI number, full street address, salary, basic IT skills ("Microsoft Word", "email"), generic interests ("socialising", "reading"), and the line "References available on request" — it is assumed.

What belongs where:
- **Experience**: paid roles, plus substantial freelance/volunteer/leadership roles if they carry target-relevant evidence. Each entry: Job title (bold) — Employer, Location | Month Year – Month Year. Title before employer unless the employer is the bigger selling point.
- **Education**: qualification, institution, dates, grade if strong (2:1 or above; omit weak grades rather than lying). Degree-level and above; drop GCSE detail once the candidate has a degree, keep "GCSEs incl. Maths and English" only if a job ad demands it.
- **Certifications**: name, issuer, year. In-progress is fine if labelled "(in progress, expected Month Year)".

## Professional summary / headline

Three to four lines, 40–80 words, directly under the header. UK convention: first person with the pronoun dropped — start with the professional identity, not "I am".

Formula: **[Identity: title/discipline + years] + [2–3 proof points with evidence, matched to the job ad's top requirements] + [what you're targeting / what you'll do for them].**

Rules:
- Write it LAST, after the bullets exist — it is a trailer for evidence that appears below, never a claim that appears nowhere else.
- Mirror the job ad's own nouns for the top 2–3 requirements (also feeds ATS keyword matching).
- At least one number.
- Ban list: "hard-working", "team player", "results-driven", "dynamic", "passionate", "proven track record", "works well independently and in a team" — any phrase that is pure assertion with no evidence attached.
- An optional one-line headline above the summary ("Data Analyst | SQL · Python · Power BI") is useful when the candidate's last job title doesn't match the target title.

Before/after:
- Weak: "Hard-working and passionate professional with excellent communication skills seeking a challenging new role."
- Strong: "Data analyst with 4 years' experience turning messy operational data into decisions in logistics and retail. Built SQL/Power BI reporting used weekly by 30+ managers; automated pipelines that cut month-end reporting from 3 days to 4 hours. Seeking senior analyst role with ownership of stakeholder-facing insight."

## Skills section

Purpose: a 3–6 line scannable block that front-loads keyword evidence. It supplements the experience bullets, never replaces them.

Rules:
- Cluster by type, ordered by relevance to the target role. Typical clusters: **Technical/analytical** (methods: statistical modelling, forecasting, process mapping), **Tools** (named software: Python, SQL, Excel, Tableau, SAP), **Languages/Certifications** where applicable.
- Keep hard skills and tools in this section. Soft skills (leadership, communication, stakeholder management) do NOT get bare list entries — they get demonstrated inside experience bullets ("Presented monthly findings to the executive board"). A listed soft skill is an unverifiable assertion; recruiters skip it.
- Every skill listed must be defensible in an interview and, ideally, visible somewhere in the bullets. Never add a skill just because the job ad names it.
- Match the job ad's exact spelling for keywords ("Power BI" not "PowerBI"; include both "machine learning" and "ML" if space allows).
- 8–15 items total. A 40-item skills dump signals indiscriminate padding and buries the signal.
- Indicate depth only where useful: "Python (pandas, scikit-learn — daily use)", "German (B2)". No skill bars, star ratings, or percentages — meaningless and ATS-hostile.

## Quantifying impact when there's no obvious number

Numbers are proof; prose is assertion. When the candidate "doesn't have numbers", extract them with these proxies:

- **Scale/scope**: budget managed, team size, number of clients/users/sites/SKUs, dataset size. "Coordinated logistics across 12 UK sites."
- **Frequency/volume**: how often, how many, per what period. "Processed 200+ samples weekly."
- **Before/after**: the comparison IS the metric. "Cut onboarding from 10 days to 4." "Reduced errors from ~1 in 20 to near zero."
- **Time**: how fast delivered, deadline beaten, duration sustained. "Delivered 3 weeks ahead of a fixed regulatory deadline."
- **Percentage**: use when the absolute number is small or confidential. "Grew newsletter sign-ups 60%."
- **Team/stakeholder count**: people trained, departments coordinated, seniority reached. "Trained 8 new starters; presented quarterly to directors."
- **Stakes**: value of what depended on the work. "Sole analyst supporting a £2m contract renewal."
- **Ranges and estimates**: legitimate when exact figures are unknown but defensible — "40–50 calls per day", "~£10k annual saving". Prefix genuine estimates with "~".
- **Selectivity/rank**: "1 of 3 selected from 40 applicants", "top-rated seller in region".

Not everything needs a number — aim for a metric in roughly two-thirds of bullets. A bullet with a strong qualitative outcome ("adopted as the standard template company-wide") beats a forced fake percentage. If no proxy exists, state the concrete deliverable and who used it.

## Length, whitespace, scan-ability

- Two pages maximum; one page acceptable for <5 years' experience. Never spill three lines onto a third page — cut instead. Never shrink font or margins to cram: cut content.
- Recruiter first pass is seconds, not minutes. The top third of page one must carry the targeting: name, headline, summary, skills, and the start of the most relevant role.
- Font: one standard face (Calibri, Arial, Helvetica, Garamond), 10.5–12pt body, 13–16pt headings. Margins ≥1.5cm. Consistent spacing between sections.
- Single-column layout. No tables, text boxes, columns, graphics, icons, photos, skill bars, or headers/footers containing real content — UK ATS parsers routinely mangle all of these.
- Section headings: plain, conventional names ("Experience", not "My Journey") in a consistent style. Bold for job titles, regular for employers, right-aligned or inline dates — pick one pattern and never vary it.
- Bullets, not paragraphs, everywhere except the summary.
- Dates as Month Year – Month Year, consistently formatted. "Present" for current role.
- Export to PDF with a professional filename ("Jane-Okafor-CV.pdf") unless the application demands DOCX.
- Zero typos. One spelling error is enough for rejection at many firms. Check UK spellings explicitly — US autocorrect is the usual culprit ("analyzed" → "analysed").

## Reverse-chronological structure

- Most recent role first, always. Within a role, most impressive bullet first.
- Every role dated; unexplained gaps over ~3 months invite the worst assumption (handling: see career-narrative.md).
- Weight space by relevance × recency: current/target-relevant roles get 4–6 bullets; a 15-year-old off-target job gets one line or is summarised ("Earlier roles: retail and hospitality positions, 2008–2012").
- Roles older than ~10–15 years: compress or cut unless they carry unique target-relevant evidence.
- Promotions within one employer: one employer heading, stacked titles with dates — shows progression without looking like job-hopping.
- Multiple concurrent tracks (e.g. part-time role + freelance): keep the chronology intact; a "Freelance & consulting" grouped entry with an overall date range is acceptable.

## Tailoring discipline (applies to every rule above)

- Read the job ad; extract the top 5–8 requirements. Each must be evidenced in the summary, skills block, or a bullet — provided the candidate genuinely has it.
- Reorder bullets within roles so the target-relevant ones lead. Rewrite bullets to use the ad's vocabulary where the evidence fits it ("stakeholder management" vs "liaising with clients").
- Cut ruthlessly: material that doesn't serve this application is noise that dilutes what does. A tailored 2-page CV always beats a complete 3-page one.
