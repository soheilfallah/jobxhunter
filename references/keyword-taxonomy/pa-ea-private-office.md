# Keyword taxonomy — PA / EA / private office (UK, entry–mid)

Use this as a KEYWORD PALETTE: match JD terms to profile evidence; flag missing must-haves as gaps.

**Weighting:** `W3` TITLE-MATCH · `W2` MUST-HAVE (essential) · `W1` NICE-TO-HAVE (desirable) ·
`[KO]` common KNOCKOUT.

Scope: private PA to a HNWI/UHNWI, executive assistant, private-office and family-office support,
lifestyle and household administration, property-portfolio administration, AI-enabled virtual
assistant. Junior-to-mid.

**Sector FLAVOUR matters more here than in any other family.** A private household, a family office,
a corporate EA desk and an agency temp brief read almost nothing alike — detect which one the advert
is and re-weight before drafting. See the cheat-sheet below.

---

## HARD SKILLS
- `W2` Complex diary management / calendar management / appointment management — the universal
  must-have; fast-changing, multi-party, protecting the principal's time
- `W2` Inbox and correspondence management — screening, prioritising, drafting on their behalf
- `W2` Travel planning and itinerary coordination — UK and international, end to end, plus the
  logistics before, during and after
- `W2` Discretion and confidentiality — handling private personal and business matters. In this
  family it is a genuine competency, not a soft skill
- `W2` Document management — organising, digitising, version control, secure backup, retrieval
- `W2` Prioritisation across competing demands; working to deadline without supervision
- `W1` Expense management, invoice processing, basic bookkeeping, budget tracking
- `W1` Meeting coordination — agendas, papers, minutes, follow-up actions
- `W1` Research-led decision support — comparing options, presenting a recommendation
- `W1` Event planning and coordination — private and corporate
- `W1` Household operations — coordinating domestic staff, contractors, suppliers, maintenance
- `W1` Property administration — tenancies, repairs, works projects, professional advisers
- `W1` Project and ad-hoc business support
- `W1` Process improvement / automating routine admin

## TOOLS / TECH
- `W2` Microsoft Office — Outlook, Word, Excel, PowerPoint. Excel to a genuinely advanced level is a
  differentiator, not a given
- `W1` Google Workspace — Gmail, Calendar, Drive, Docs, Sheets
- `W1` Microsoft 365 / SharePoint / Teams / OneDrive
- `W1` Notion, Asana, Trello, Monday, Slack — task and comms tooling
- `W1` DocuSign, Dropbox, expense platforms (Concur, Expensify), travel booking portals
- `W1` CRM or database hygiene — data entry, validation, keeping records current
- `W1` AI assistants and workflow automation used daily — a real and currently uncommon
  differentiator in this family; lead with it only where the advert signals appetite for it

## CERTIFICATIONS / LICENCES
- `W1` Full UK driving licence — frequently essential for private household and estate roles
- `W1` Valid passport and willingness to travel at short notice
- `W1` First aid (EFAW) — genuinely relevant in a private household with a family
- `W1` DBS check — expect it for household roles with children
- `W1` Right to work in the UK — often an application-form field. Keep it OFF the CV unless a form
  explicitly asks

## GENUINE SOFT SKILLS
Only claim these where a bullet demonstrates them; never as an adjective block.
- `W2` Proactivity — anticipating needs before being asked
- `W2` Attention to detail and accuracy
- `W2` Calm under pressure; adaptable when the day changes
- `W2` Trustworthiness — this family hires on trust first and skills second
- `W1` Warm, personable communication; polished written and spoken English
- `W1` Ownership and initiative; comfortable deciding without escalating
- `W1` "Roll up your sleeves" practicality — private households say this constantly and mean it

## TITLE VARIANTS
`W3` on title match:
- Personal Assistant / Private PA / PA to a HNWI or UHNWI
- Executive Assistant / EA to the CEO, Founder, Partner or Director
- Private Office Assistant / Family Office Assistant / Personal & Business Assistant
- Lifestyle Manager / Household Manager / House Manager / Estate Manager
- Property Portfolio Administrator / Private Office Administrator
- Team Assistant / Business Support / Operations & Executive Support
- Virtual Assistant / AI-enabled Virtual Assistant
- Chief of Staff (junior) — only where the advert is genuinely EA-shaped

## COMMON KNOCKOUTS
- `[KO]` "Extensive" or a stated minimum of years supporting a HNWI or private family
- `[KO]` Managing **domestic / household staff** — a distinct competency; supervising staff in any
  other setting is NOT the same thing and must not be presented as if it were
- `[KO]` **Events at the highest level** — a specialism, not a nice-to-have, where an advert says
  an events background is ideal
- `[KO]` An established local network of suppliers, contractors and professional advisers
- `[KO]` Sector-specific EA background — private equity, financial services, law, entertainment
- `[KO]` Already based in a named area, or able to commute to a specific private address daily
- `[KO]` Second language for an international principal
- `[KO]` Shorthand, or a stated minimum typing speed (older corporate adverts)
- `[KO]` Willingness to travel with the principal for extended periods

## Sector-flavour cheat-sheet (re-weight the must-haves by setting)
| Setting | What actually gets you shortlisted |
|---|---|
| **Private household** | Trust, discretion, household running, domestic staff, contractors, driving licence, being local. Warmth and fit with the family matter as much as the CV |
| **Family office** | Discretion plus business support — documents, correspondence, travel, professional advisers, some financial admin |
| **Corporate EA** | Board-level diary, stakeholder management, papers and decks, sector fluency, polish |
| **Property / private office** | Liaison with solicitors, surveyors, architects, accountants; works and maintenance coordination; spreadsheets and records |
| **Agency / temp brief** | Speed, immediate availability, and evidence you have done the exact duty list before. The agency is screening for a fee, so it screens hard |

## Notes for the tailorer
- **Trust is the product.** The strongest opening is what the principal stopped having to think
  about once the candidate took it on — not a duty list. Duty lists are what everyone else sends.
- **Do not inflate the relationship.** "Single point of contact", "trusted right hand" and
  "ran his personal life" are strong *and* checkable. Inflated seniority is caught in one question.
- **Confidentiality is part of the pitch, so honour it on the page.** Where an engagement is private,
  say "a high-net-worth private client" or "a confidential private client" — never name the
  principal, their employer, or **their profession**. Naming the profession narrows identification
  and undercuts the very discretion being claimed.
- **Household staff, events and supplier networks are the three things most candidates lack.** If
  the profile does not evidence them, leave them out silently — do not stretch adjacent supervision
  into "managed domestic staff".
- Part-time, freelance and self-employed briefs are common in this family and are often a *better*
  fit for a portfolio career. Say plainly that the basis suits, rather than treating it as a caveat.
- AI and automation genuinely differentiate here, because so few PA candidates have it. Land it as
  quiet efficiency ("cut the time routine correspondence took"), not as a technology pitch — unless
  the advert asks for it, in which case lead with it.
