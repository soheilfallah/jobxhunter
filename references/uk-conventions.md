# UK CV Conventions — Reference

You are tailoring CVs for the UK market. Apply every rule below unless the job
description (JD) explicitly overrides it. When UK and US conventions conflict, UK wins.

## Core norms

- Call it a **CV**, never "résumé" or "resume" — in the document, the filename,
  and the cover letter. Do not write "Curriculum Vitae" as a heading either;
  the candidate's name is the heading.
- **Length: two A4 pages maximum** for a standard CV.
  - One page for students/early-career with thin experience.
  - One page is *mandatory in practice* for junior investment banking.
  - Only academic CVs run longer (3–5+ pages; see sector deltas).
  - Never pad to fill page two. A strong 1.5 pages beats a diluted 2.
- **A4 paper size**, not US Letter. Export as PDF unless the JD demands .docx.
- **Never include**:
  - photo (acting/modelling only)
  - date of birth or age
  - marital status or dependants
  - nationality as identity (right-to-work status is different; see below)
  - gender
  - full home address (city is enough)
  - National Insurance number
  UK anti-discrimination practice means these actively hurt, not just waste space.
- **Reverse-chronological is the default structure.** Use a skills-led/functional
  CV only to manage a career change or a long gap — and even then keep a dated
  work history section; UK recruiters distrust CVs with no visible timeline.
- **Personal profile at the top**: 3–4 lines directly under the contact block.
  - Formula: who they are + strongest relevant evidence + what they're targeting.
  - Rewrite it for every application; it must echo the JD's own vocabulary.
  - No "I": write "Data engineer with 4 years..." not "I am a data engineer...".
  - Ban clichés: "hard-working team player", "dynamic", "results-driven",
    "thinking outside the box". Replace each with a concrete fact.
- Standard section order (experienced hire):
  1. Name + contact details (+ right-to-work line if applicable)
  2. Personal profile
  3. Work experience (reverse-chronological)
  4. Education
  5. Skills (move above Education for tech; above Experience for career changers)
  6. Optional: projects, certifications, interests (only if they carry signal)
  Students/new graduates: Education before Experience.

## Style and formatting

- **UK spelling throughout**: organised, analyse, programme (but "program" for
  software), centre, labour, behaviour, optimised, fulfil, specialise,
  licence (noun) / license (verb). Audit the final draft for US spellings —
  they read as an untailored American template.
- **UK date style**: "Sept 2024 – Present", "March 2021 – June 2023", or
  "September 2024". Month + year is enough. Never numeric US order (09/15/2024).
  En dash with spaces for ranges. Be consistent about abbreviated vs full months.
- **Bullets, not prose.** Start each with a strong verb (led, built, delivered,
  reduced, negotiated); quantify wherever possible; 1–2 lines per bullet.
- 3–6 bullets for recent/relevant roles; 1–2 for older or less relevant ones.
- Font 10–11pt minimum, one clean typeface. Job title and organisation
  left-aligned, dates right-aligned. Generous white space.
- No tables, text boxes, columns, icons, or graphics that break ATS parsing.
  No skill-rating bars or star ratings — UK recruiters mock them.
- Cut generic filler:
  - "References available on request"
  - basic IT ("Microsoft Word", "email", "internet")
  - hobbies like "socialising, reading, travel"
  Interests earn a line only when they carry signal: a blog for a writing role,
  competitive sport for a sales culture, open-source work for engineering.
- Handle employment gaps of 6+ months with one honest line ("Career break for
  caring responsibilities, 2022–2023") rather than silence — unexplained gaps
  trigger screening questions anyway.
- One typo can kill an application; UK careers services say this explicitly.
  Run a final proofread pass specifically against UK spelling and date format.

## Contact details

Single line or compact block at the top:

- Full name (large — it is the de facto document title).
- Phone in UK format: `+44 7XXX XXXXXX` if the candidate is abroad or targeting
  international recruiters; `07XXX XXXXXX` for a purely domestic search.
- Professional email (firstname.lastname@ style; no dated or jokey handles).
- LinkedIn URL with a cleaned custom slug.
- **Location as city/region only** — "Manchester, UK" — never a full postal
  address. UK employers screen for commutability, so if relocating write
  "Relocating to London, September 2026" rather than hiding location.
- Tech roles: add the GitHub/portfolio link here.

Example:

```
Sara Ahmadi
Manchester, UK · +44 7700 900123 · sara.ahmadi@gmail.com
linkedin.com/in/sara-ahmadi · github.com/sahmadi
```

## Right to work / visa status

Sponsorship is a legal knockout in UK recruiting — many employers cannot or
will not sponsor, and ATS forms ask the question directly. Handle it; never
hide it.

- **Include a right-to-work line when any of these hold**:
  - the JD mentions right to work, sponsorship, or security clearance;
  - the CV signals a non-UK background (foreign degrees, overseas employers)
    that would otherwise make screeners assume sponsorship is needed;
  - the candidate genuinely needs sponsorship.
  Otherwise omit it — UK citizens with UK-only histories don't need the line.
- Placement: one line directly under the contact details, plainly labelled.
- Phrasings by scenario:
  - Full, permanent rights (ILR, settled status, citizenship):
    `Right to work in the UK — no sponsorship required`
    Do not name the immigration category; the status is the employer's business,
    the route is not.
  - Time-limited visa with full work rights:
    `Right to work: Graduate Route visa, full-time work permitted (valid to Oct 2027)`
    Include the expiry only when it is comfortably long; recruiters will ask regardless.
  - Needs sponsorship:
    `Eligible for Skilled Worker visa sponsorship`
    State it honestly and early. Hiding it wastes a licensed sponsor's time and
    burns the relationship. Target employers on GOV.UK's register of licensed
    sponsors and roles meeting Skilled Worker salary thresholds.
- Never write "Nationality: X". Work authorisation is the only immigration fact
  an employer may lawfully weigh, so it is the only one the CV states.

## References / referees

- **Do not list referees on a standard CV.**
- **Drop "References available on request".** It is redundant — employers assume
  it — and spends a line better used on evidence. Most UK employers take
  references only at (conditional) offer stage. The National Careers Service
  still tolerates the phrase; university careers services and recruiters say
  cut it. Follow the latter: it is current practice.
- Keep a separate one-page referee sheet (names, titles, relationship, contact
  details) ready to send when asked — never embedded in the CV.
- **Exceptions**:
  - Academic CVs: list 2–3 referees with titles, affiliations, and emails.
    Choose people known in the field; get their permission first.
  - NHS/Trac and Civil Service application *forms* require referee details
    covering the last ~3 years — that lives in the form, not the CV.

## Education — UK presentation

- Reverse-chronological: degree, institution, dates, classification/grade.
- Placement: lead with Education only for students, recent graduates, or
  academic roles; otherwise it sits below Experience.
- **UK degree classifications**: First (1st), Upper Second (2:1), Lower Second
  (2:2), Third. Write it inline: "BSc (Hons) Computer Science, 2:1".
  - State the classification when it is a First or 2:1.
  - Omit a 2:2/Third rather than advertise it — but never lie; verification is routine.
  - Many graduate schemes still screen at 2:1, though some have dropped it.
- Masters grades are Distinction / Merit / Pass — state Distinction or Merit.
- **A-levels/GCSEs**: include for students and graduates within ~2–3 years of
  graduating (grad schemes often want UCAS evidence):
  - `A-levels: Maths (A*), Physics (A), Chemistry (A)`
  - GCSEs in one line: `9 GCSEs at grades 9–6 (A*–B), incl. Maths and English`
  - Drop both once there is a degree plus meaningful work history.
- Add one line of degree substance when relevant to the JD: dissertation title,
  key modules, prizes, scholarship.

### International qualifications (first user: Iranian BSc + MSc, plus a UK MSc)

- **Lead with the UK degree.** It anchors the candidate in the UK system and
  implicitly evidences English and UK academic standards. Give it the fullest
  treatment: classification, dissertation, relevant modules.
- Present each foreign degree with an explicit UK frame so the screener never
  has to guess:

```
MSc Data Science — University of Manchester, UK                    2023 – 2024
Distinction. Dissertation: "..." (supervised by ...)

MSc Electrical Engineering — Sharif University of Technology, Iran 2018 – 2020
GPA 17.8/20, ranked 3rd of 120 (equivalent to UK Masters with Distinction)

BSc Computer Engineering — University of Tehran, Iran              2014 – 2018
GPA 16.4/20, top 10% of cohort (equivalent to UK 2:1 BSc (Hons))
```

- Translate degree titles into English; keep the institution's real name —
  Sharif, Tehran, and Amirkabir carry weight with informed reviewers.
- **UK ENIC (Ecctis) Statements of Comparability confirm the *level* of a
  qualification ("comparable to British Bachelor (Honours) degree standard")
  but NOT the grade.** If the candidate holds one, add "UK ENIC confirmed" or
  offer it on request — it removes screening friction, especially for regulated
  sectors and grad schemes.
- Because ENIC doesn't certify grades, any "2:1 equivalent" claim is a reasoned
  self-assessment: always back it with the raw GPA and rank, never assert it bare.
- Iranian 20-point scale, rough banding used by UK university admissions:
  ~17+/20 ≈ First/Distinction band; ~15–17 ≈ 2:1/Merit band. Institutions vary —
  always show the underlying number alongside the claimed band.
- Never leave a foreign grade untranslated ("GPA: 17.8" alone means nothing to
  a UK screener) and never omit the country.

## Sector deltas

The baseline above holds everywhere; shift the weighting per sector.

- **Finance (IB / markets / PE)**
  - One page, hard limit, below VP level. Conservative format, zero graphics.
  - Quantify everything in £/$/%: deal size, book size, P&L, cost saved.
  - Brand names are screened first — bank, target university, classification.
    Put them where the eye lands (left column, bold).
  - A-levels/UCAS points are still expected on junior finance CVs.
  - Mirror each bank's own language; sprayed generic CVs get rejected.
- **Consulting (MBB / Big 4)**
  - 1–2 pages, but every line must be an achievement with a number; screeners
    judge impact-per-line and structured thinking.
  - Leadership and extracurricular evidence weighs more than in finance.
  - Expect 2:1+ classification screening; state classification prominently.
- **Tech / startups**
  - Skills section near the top: languages, frameworks, cloud — mirror the JD's
    stack exactly for ATS keyword matching.
  - GitHub/portfolio link is quasi-mandatory for engineers; most tech recruiters
    check it. Ensure 2–3 pinned, well-documented repos beat many stale ones.
  - A Projects section can outrank Education for career changers and bootcamp grads.
  - Startups tolerate personality and non-linear paths; big tech wants scale
    metrics (users, latency, uptime, cost).
- **Academia**
  - A different genre: 3–5+ pages, no hard limit.
  - Order: contacts → research interests → qualifications → then *reorder by
    post*: research-led roles put publications and grants first; teaching-led
    roles put teaching first.
  - Full publications list in a consistent citation style, own name highlighted;
    conferences; grants with amounts and funders; teaching with level and role;
    professional service (peer review, editorial, committees); referees listed.
  - Note the funder-driven shift to **narrative CVs** (Résumé for Research and
    Innovation, R4RI): contribution and context over raw output counts. Check
    the funder's or institution's required format before defaulting to classic.
- **NHS / public sector**
  - NHS hiring runs through NHS Jobs/Trac *forms*, not free CVs. The scored
    section is **"supporting information"**: address every essential (and
    ideally desirable) criterion of the **person specification**, in order,
    with one concrete evidenced example each. Do not paste the CV in.
  - Use values-based language (NHS Constitution values, the Trust's own values);
    panels score against them.
  - Medical/dental posts may still request a CV in NHS format: GMC number,
    qualifications, clinical experience by rotation, audits/QI, CPD, teaching.
  - Civil Service uses Success Profiles: a ~2-page CV plus separate behaviour
    statements (250-word examples). Write the statements to the named
    behaviours; the CV alone is not the scored artefact.

## Tailoring workflow (apply every time)

1. Extract from the JD: must-have skills, sponsorship stance, classification
   threshold, sector, any stated format rules.
2. Rewrite the personal profile to answer that JD in its own vocabulary.
3. Reorder bullets so the top two bullets of the top role hit the JD's top two
   requirements.
4. Set length and structure by sector: 1 page finance/junior; 2 pages default;
   long-form academic; form-based NHS/Civil Service (write the supporting
   information/behaviours, not just the CV).
5. Add or remove the right-to-work line per the rules above.
6. Final pass: UK spelling, UK dates, no photo/DOB/full address, no
   "references available on request", zero typos.
