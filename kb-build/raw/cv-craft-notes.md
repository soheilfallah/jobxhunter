# Raw research notes — CV craft & career narrative (gathered 2026-07-06)

Working notes only. Distilled outputs live in ../../references/. Sources verified via web search/fetch.

## UK format norms
- Two pages A4 is the UK standard; one page fine for early-career. Academic CVs exempt. (Oxford, Prospects, standout-cv, cvengineer)
- No photo, no date of birth, no marital status, no nationality, no NI number, no "Curriculum Vitae" heading, no full postal address (city + postcode enough). References on request — omit the line entirely per most modern guides; Oxford says exclude referees except academic CVs.
- Section order (experienced hire): contact → profile/summary → (key skills) → experience reverse-chron → education → additional (languages, volunteering, interests if relevant).
- Students/recent grads: education before experience (Oxford default order for students).
- ATS: single column, standard fonts, no text boxes/graphics/tables/headers-footers for key info, clear standard headings, PDF unless DOCX requested. Many UK ATS can't parse multi-column. (brendanhope, resumemate, cvbuilderai)
- British English spelling; first person without "I" ("Finance manager with…").
- Oxford: 10–11pt minimum font, full page width, scannable at arm's length, zero typos ("recruiters dismiss even one error").

## Bullet anatomy
- Google XYZ (Laszlo Bock): "Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y], by doing [Z]". Results-first. Example: "Increased sales by 25% by launching a new line of business in Q1." (tealhq, sweresume, wonsulting)
- Oxford CAR: Context / Action words / Results. One idea per bullet.
- STAR (Situation-Task-Action-Result) — same DNA; on the page compress to Action+Result, keep S/T for interviews.
- Start with strong action verb, no pronouns, past tense for past roles.
- Edinburgh/Prospects: don't list tasks; match each task to a demonstrable skill; achievement-focused.
- Keep bullets ~1 line, max 2; 3–6 bullets per recent role, fewer for old roles.

## Quantification proxies (The Muse, ResumeWorded, resume.io, Indeed, Zippia)
- Axes: scale/scope (budget, team size, users, clients served), volume (items processed, reports delivered), frequency (per day/week), time (how fast, how often), before/after comparison, rankings, ranges when exact numbers unknown ("40–50 calls/day"), stakes (revenue at risk, compliance deadline), engagement (attendees, followers).
- Ranges are legitimate when precise figures aren't available; estimates fine if defensible.
- Never invent numbers. Quantify honestly or use scope words.

## Summary/profile (Prospects, Indeed UK, Zety, myperfectcv)
- 3–5 sentences / ~50–80 words. Who you are (title + years) → 2-3 proof points with evidence → what you're aiming at, matched to the role.
- Kill: "hard-working team player", "works well independently and in a team", buzzword strings with no evidence.
- Mirror the job description's language; tailored per application.
- UK convention: first person, dropped pronoun.

## Skills section
- Cluster: technical/hard skills vs tools vs languages; soft skills shown in bullets, not listed bare (widespread recruiter advice; Edinburgh/Prospects: evidence over assertion).
- Order clusters by relevance to target role; keywords for ATS come from the job ad.

## Format choice for career changers
- ~90% of recruiters prefer reverse-chronological (Jobscan, career.io). Functional/skills-only CVs make recruiters suspicious — look like hiding gaps or thin history (Jobscan, Indeed).
- Hybrid/combination = lead with profile + skills evidence block, keep dated reverse-chron history below. Recommended for career changers (Randstad, Nottingham careers blog, career.io).
- Oxford: skills-based only with substantial experience or career change, needs extensive evidence.

## Gaps (National Careers Service, Robert Walters, Hays, Indeed UK, CV Centre)
- Never leave unexplained gaps ≥ ~3–6 months; recruiters assume the worst.
- One honest line, no over-explanation: "2021–2022: Parental leave"; "Career break — relocation and full-time caring responsibility".
- Health: "time out to deal with a health issue, now resolved" — no detail owed; illegal for employer to demand.
- Redundancy: frame as business decision on the role, not the person.
- Include anything productive in the gap: courses, freelance, volunteering, skills maintained.
- Years-only dates can soften small gaps but reads evasive if used to hide big ones; month+year is the honest default.

## Red thread / career narrative
- "Red thread" (rode draad / røde tråd): the single organising through-line connecting disparate roles. Tamsen Webster's storytelling framing; Interview Guys "career narrative statement"; Columbia SPS storytelling tips; Kamerow Medium piece.
- Rules distilled: narrative doesn't need every job; cut impressive-but-off-thread material; make the journey look intentional — each move builds on the last; 30-second version should exist.
- Work backwards from the target role: pick the thread the TARGET cares about, then re-read history through that lens.

## Academia → data science reframe (Towards Data Science, cheekyscientist, jellis18, UMich career center, Academic Positions)
- PhD transferables: experiment design, statistical analysis, handling messy data, programming (R/Python), version control, literature synthesis → research skills, communicating technical results to non-technical audiences, project ownership, grant/budget management.
- Failure mode: CVs written for academics (publications first, jargon, no business language). Fix: lead with skills the employer names, translate outputs (papers → "communicated complex analyses to non-specialist audiences"), quantify datasets/pipelines.
- Mindset: industry wants broad-and-applied over deep-and-narrow.
