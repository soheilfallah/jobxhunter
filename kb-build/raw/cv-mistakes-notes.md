# Raw research notes — CV failure modes (for references/cv-mistakes.md)

Collected 2026-07-06 via web search + fetch. UK-first framing.

## Buzzwords / corporate slop

- Novoresume "79 Resume Buzzwords to Avoid" (https://novoresume.com/career-blog/resume-buzzwords-to-avoid) — fetched:
  - 30 overused personality traits (ambitious, dedicated, detail-oriented, driven, dynamic, enthusiastic, excellent communicator, fast learner, flexible, forward-thinking, go-getter, goal-oriented, hard worker, innovative, motivated, natural leader, organized, passionate, people person, proactive, problem-solver, reliable, results-driven, self-starter, team player) — "don't prove your interpersonal skills or achievements", everyone claims them.
  - 21 weak action verbs (responsible for, helped with, assisted, worked on, participated in, tasked with, handled, dealt with, supported, involved in, carried out, performed, facilitated...) — "fail to show what the candidate actually accomplished".
  - 13 outdated terms (objective statement, duties included, seasoned professional, references available upon request, Microsoft Office proficient, thinking outside the box, synergy) — signal candidate hasn't kept up; waste space.
  - 15 unnecessarily complex words (leverage, synergy, utilize, paradigm, robust, core competency, disrupt, value-added, mission-critical) — jargon; simple clear language more effective.
- Interview Guys / Teal / Interspect / Connors Group / recruiters.ie search digest:
  - "team player", "results-driven", "detail-oriented", "hardworking", "problem solver", "go-getter", "think outside the box", "synergy", "people person", "dynamic" — trigger eye-rolls; recruiters see them hundreds of times weekly; vague, unverifiable, make other claims less credible; zero ATS keyword value (no JD lists them as requirements).
  - Specific/functional verbs ("optimised", "implemented", "facilitated") outperform trait adjectives.

## AI-slop tells (2025–26)

- 53% of recruiters say they can tell AI-written CVs (ResuFit, https://resufit.com/blog/can-recruiters-tell-if-you-used-ai-to-write-your-resume/).
- Tell-tale AI verbs repeating across applications: "spearheaded", "championed", "orchestrated", "leveraged"; stock phrases "collaborated cross-functionally", "drove strategic initiatives", "leveraged data to inform decisions". Senior-register verbs on junior CVs = red flag.
- AI text is polished but nameless: no actual tools, clients, constraints, or oddball specifics. Hiring managers reject "reads like ChatGPT" on sight (~10-second read).
- URLs: https://www.willo.video/blog/how-to-detect-ai-generated-resumes ; https://enhancv.com/blog/signs-of-ai-generated-resume/ ; https://www.huffpost.com/entry/recruiters-job-application-chatgpt_l_6723b4e1e4b0871068fe81ad ; https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/employers-can-tell-if-you-used-chatgpt-to-write-your-resume/478444

## StandOut CV — 39 CV mistakes (https://standout-cv.com/cv-advice/cv-mistakes) — fetched, full list

High damage: not tailoring (No.1, most damaging); thin detail in current role (recruiters read latest role hardest); basic language; wrong role order (must be reverse-chronological); unexplained gaps; grammar; spelling; lies; template instructions left in.
Medium: wild unproven claims ("Best salesman in Europe"); big text chunks; not showing impact (example: "Producing financial reports" → "Producing financial reports, resulting in £10k annual saving"); keyword stuffing; no high-level role summary; meaningless clichés; elaborate fonts; crazy colours; skills graphs (no scale — replace with "3 years HTML", certs, task scale); inconsistent fonts; word repetition; too many pages (norm: ~two pages); third-person narration; strange interests; padding; pronoun overuse; unclear sections.
Low: ridiculous email address; reasons for leaving; logos/images (confuse scanning software); too much detail in old roles (short summaries for 10-year-old roles); salary details; messy page transitions; photos (UK: exclude); reference details (offer stage only); poor file naming ("Your Name CV"); wasted space; DOB/age (exclude — bias + ID theft); full address (town/city only); non-text formats (keep Word/PDF).

## Coburg Banks — 22 reasons CV rejected (https://www.coburgbanks.co.uk/blog/22-reasons-your-cv-will-get-rejected) — fetched

Ridiculous email; spelling & grammar (careless/lazy); inaccurate/missing dates (suggests hiding something); candidate photo; formatting fumbles (breaks on job boards); War & Peace length; long paragraphs; TMI personal details; lies (background checks); silly fonts/colours; generic CV; unexplained gaps; wrong chronological order; chancing your arm (unsuitable applications); no employer context (say what the company does); weird hobbies; meaningless "hard-working" intros; missing contact details; third person.

## UK university careers services

- Reading careers blog top 10 (https://blogs.reading.ac.uk/careers/the-top-10-cv-mistakes/) — fetched: font 11+, business-like, consistent; professional email; "Most CVs should be two full pages" (IB may want 1 page; academic CVs 3+ with publications); tailoring via employer's language; degree detail 6–8 lines incl. dissertation-as-project; unsupported claims need evidence; bullet structure; omit age/gender/marital status/nationality/photo for UK roles; brief referees or "available on request"; proofread.
- Warwick (https://warwick.ac.uk/services/careers/blog/four_common_mistakes_to_avoid_when_creating_your_cv/), Liverpool, Sunderland London, Greenwich (search digest): generic CVs spotted instantly; unsubstantiated claims ("passionate", "strategic", "enthusiastic" without proof = less credible); UK English spelling matters (US spellings can read as errors); avoid tables/text boxes (ATS can't read them); no photo in UK (discrimination-law norm); strange email addresses question judgement.

## ATS mechanics (kept brief in deliverable; details live in ats-mechanics.md)

- Jobscan (https://www.jobscan.co/blog/ats-formatting-mistakes/ ; https://www.jobscan.co/blog/resume-tables-columns-ats/): parsers read linearly L→R, top→bottom; tables/columns cause "text-layer scrambling" (parser slices horizontally across cells → word salad). Headers/footers ignored by many scrapers — TopResume research: contact info missed 25% of the time when in header/footer. Custom fonts/icons parse as gibberish/[NULL]. Standard fonts: Arial, Calibri, Georgia.
- Also: https://www.resumemate.io/blog/tables-columns-text-boxes-do-they-break-ats-safer-layouts/ ; https://eliteresumes.co/career-resources/ats-optimization/ats-formatting.html

## Tense & dates

- Consensus across Indeed, Resume.io, StandOut CV, Teal, Resume Worded: present tense for current role's ongoing duties; past tense for previous roles and for completed achievements even within current role; never mix tenses within one bullet/section.
- Consistent date format throughout (e.g. "Mar 2022 – Present"); missing/inaccurate dates read as concealment (Coburg Banks).
- URLs: https://standout-cv.com/cv-advice/cv-tense ; https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/resume-tense ; https://resume.io/blog/resume-tenses ; https://www.tealhq.com/post/resume-past-or-present-tense

## Quantification / achievements-vs-duties stats

- 2023 CV-Library survey: 92% of UK hiring managers said quantifiable achievements were the most convincing CV element vs 8% valuing responsibilities alone (via omyresumes.co.uk digest).
- 40% of recruiters: biggest jobseeker mistake = not quantifying accomplishments; 34%: lack of result statements is a dealbreaker; numbers can raise interview chances ~40%; 75% of hiring managers want quantified achievements in work experience (Zippia/Enhancv/Novoresume stats round-ups).
- Metric types: percentages (growth/savings), £ values, timeframes, volumes. Percentages compare; raw numbers add scale.
- URLs: https://www.zippia.com/advice/resume-statistics/ ; https://enhancv.com/blog/resume-statistics/ ; https://novoresume.com/career-blog/resume-statistics ; https://resumeworded.com/how-to-quantify-resume-key-advice ; https://omyresumes.co.uk/achievements-vs-responsibilities-what-uk-recruiters-really-want-on-your-cv/

## Scan-time / attention

- Ladders 2018 eye-tracking: 7.4 seconds initial screen (https://www.theladders.com/static/images/basicSite/pdfs/TheLadders-EyeTracking-StudyC2.pdf ; https://www.hrdive.com/news/eye-tracking-study-shows-recruiters-look-at-resumes-for-7-seconds/541582/).
- F-pattern scanning (Nielsen Norman-derived): top lines + left edge get read; front-load bullets. (https://standout-cv.com/stats/how-long-recruiters-spend-looking-at-cv)
- Counterpoint: some UK research (New College of the Humanities) found ~3m14s per CV — scan first, read if it survives.

## Action-verb allow-lists (UK sources)

- Indeed UK (https://uk.indeed.com/career-advice/cvs-cover-letters/action-verbs-to-make-your-cv-stand-out), CXK (https://www.cxk.org/resources/139-action-verbs-to-make-your-cv-stand-out/), LiveCareer UK, CV Maker UK: one strong verb per bullet + result; match verb to function (negotiated/sales, analysed/research, trained/education, streamlined/efficiency); UK spellings (analysed, organised, optimised); vary verbs.

## UK norms distilled

- Two pages is the norm (Reading, StandOut CV, Coburg Banks); 1 page acceptable for early-career/IB; 3+ only academic with publications.
- No photo, no DOB, no marital status, no full address, no NI number — Equality Act 2010 bias norms + ID theft.
- "CV" not "resume"; UK English spellings; £ not $; A4 not US Letter.
- References: "available on request" or omit entirely.
- Personal profile 3–4 lines max, tailored, no clichés.
