# Raw research notes — UK cover letters (2026-07-06)

Working notes for distillation into references/cover-letter.md. Not runtime-read.

## Structure / length (UK university careers services)

Oxford Careers Service (https://www.careers.ox.ac.uk/cover-letters):
- Business-letter format, max one page A4.
- Opening: introduce, purpose, where you found the role.
- "Why this role": tailor to org + position, research beyond the website, cite specific work/market position.
- "Why you": match 3–5 most relevant skills/experience to JD; "state explicitly how you match the job criteria".
- Closing: reiterate interest, forward-looking statement.
- Address a named person where possible; else "Dear Recruitment Manager" / "Dear Recruiter".
- Sign-off: named → Yours sincerely; unnamed → Yours faithfully.
- Mistakes: starting every sentence with "I"; repeating CV; generic letters; unsubstantiated claims; spelling errors.
- Tone: professional but conversational; clear concise English; no jargon; explain org-specific terms.

National Careers Service (https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk/careers-advice/covering-letter):
- 3–5 paragraphs. P1 intro (role, where seen, reference no., enthusiasm). P2 skills matched to requirements + org fit. P3 optional context (gaps, disability). P4 close: thanks, point to CV, contact.
- Salutation table: "Dear [Name]" → Yours sincerely; "Dear Sir or Madam" → Yours faithfully.
- Mistakes: generic letters, formatting inconsistent with CV, wrong company/recruiter names, unsupported claims, spelling/grammar.

University of Leicester (https://le.ac.uk/career-development-service/applications-and-cvs/cover-letters):
- ICME framework: Introduction, Capability, Motivation, Ending.

Edinburgh Careers (https://careers.ed.ac.uk/cvs-and-applications/writing-a-cover-letter):
- Why this type of work / why this employer / why you — three motivation strands.
- 3–5 short tailored paragraphs, one page, sans-serif 10–12pt, new letter per application.

## Sign-off rule (multiple sources)
- Named recipient ("Dear Ms Patel") → "Yours sincerely".
- Unnamed ("Dear Hiring Manager", "Dear Sir or Madam") → "Yours faithfully".
- Sources: Youth Employment UK (https://www.youthemployment.org.uk/how-to-greet-future-employers-and-sign-off-in-a-cover-letter-or-application/), National Careers Service, LiveCareer UK (https://www.livecareer.co.uk/cover-letter/how-to-address-a-cover-letter).
- Getting it wrong reads as lack of attention to detail.

## Openers / clichés
- "I am writing to apply for..." = wasted first line; blends into 100+ applications.
- TopCV UK (https://topcv.co.uk/career-advice/cut-cover-letter-cliches): cut "go-getter", "self-starter", "detail-oriented", "think outside the box", "passionate, results-driven".
- The Muse (https://www.themuse.com/advice/how-to-start-a-cover-letter-opening-lines-examples; https://www.themuse.com/advice/5-cliche-cover-letter-lines-to-avoid-at-all-costs): open with a relevant accomplishment, a specific company hook, or a concrete reason. Recruiters judge within seconds.
- Empty adjectives without an example remain empty claims.

## AI-generated letters / authenticity
- Insight Global 2025 survey: 88% of hiring managers believe they can tell AI-written applications; 54% would care. TopResume survey: 67% can spot AI letters, 54% view negatively.
- Detection tells: generic phrasing, repeated buzzwords, impersonal "corporate high-vocabulary HR speak most people don't naturally type", clone-like structure across candidates, too polished / no anecdote or perspective.
- Prospects (https://www.prospects.ac.uk/careers-advice/getting-a-job/using-generative-ai-in-job-applications/): UK employers accept AI assistance but expect final version personalised, authentic, tailored.
- Letters with personal details, conversational language, real specifics do not trip the "AI" flag.
- => This is the empirical backing for the tone-preservation rule.

## ATS
- Jobscan (https://www.jobscan.co/blog/cover-letter-robot-approved/): major ATSs (Workday, Greenhouse, Lever, Taleo, iCIMS) parse and store the cover letter; some include it in recruiter keyword searches.
- Mirror the job title exactly; work 3–5 of the posting's most repeated skills into real sentences; no keyword stuffing.
- Can't know if a given ATS scores the letter → write as if it does; costs nothing.
