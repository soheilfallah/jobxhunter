# Raw research notes — UK CV conventions (2026-07-06)

Working notes for references/uk-conventions.md. Not distilled; keep URLs.

## Length / general norms
- Prospects.ac.uk (How to write a CV): recommends no longer than two A4 pages (Univ. of Chester careers service quote). Shorter for students seeking part-time work; academic CVs slightly longer; 3 pages only for senior/very experienced.
  https://www.prospects.ac.uk/careers-advice/cvs-and-cover-letters/how-to-write-a-cv/
- Oxford Careers Service (CVs page): 1–2 full pages standard; academic CVs no page limit. Reverse chronological recommended. Never include: DOB/age, marital status, disability status, home address, the words "Curriculum Vitae", referee details (except academic), basic IT skills (word processing etc.), generic interests ("socialising with friends", "reading"). Include degree classifications where strong; give equivalent grading for non-UK qualifications; quantify achievements; 10–11pt min font; job title/org left, dates right; bullets not prose; save as PDF; one typo can sink a CV. Academic CVs: research interests early, publications, conferences, teaching, admin, referees listed (2), narrative-CV trend for funders (quality over quantity, context, contribution). North America delta: Letter size, US spelling.
  https://www.careers.ox.ac.uk/cvs
- Prospects content summary: contact details = full name, mobile, email (their older guidance says home address; Oxford + Nat. Careers Service say omit/city only). DOB irrelevant; no photo unless acting/modelling. Personal statement/profile at top: concise, key attributes + career aims. Education reverse-date. Work experience reverse-chronological, relevant only. Skills: languages, IT packages relevant to role. Interests only if they add signal (blog for journalism etc.).

## Contact details / sections
- National Careers Service (cv-sections): include name, phone, email, LinkedIn if available; exclude age, DOB, marital status, nationality, photo. Personal statement "a few short lines" just under name/contacts. Work history reverse chronological; 2–3 lines per role, STAR method. Education after intro, or earlier if little work experience. NCS still suggests "references available on request" line — NOTE: contradicts most university/recruiter advice (see below); treat as legacy.
  https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk/careers-advice/cv-sections

## References / referees
- Indeed UK: "'References Available Upon Request' and Other Phrases To Avoid" — drop the line.
  https://uk.indeed.com/career-advice/cvs-cover-letters/references-available-upon-request
- TopCV UK: don't include the phrase; employers assume references exist; ask at offer stage.
  https://topcv.co.uk/career-advice/should-i-include-references-available-upon-request-in-my-cv
- Search synthesis: convention obsolete; ~70%+ of UK employers request references only at offer stage (CIPD survey cited by secondary sources); keep a separate reference sheet ready. Oxford: non-academic CVs — no referees ("they'll assume you have them"); academic CVs — list 2–3 referees, ask permission first.
- NCS is the outlier still permitting the line — harmless but wastes space.

## Right to work / visa status
- AlignCV (Skilled Worker visa CV guide): be upfront; put right-to-work status directly below contact details on page 1. Examples: "Right to Work: Requires Skilled Worker Visa Sponsorship" / "Right to Work: Graduate Route visa (valid until Oct 2028), no immediate sponsorship required."
  https://getaligncv.com/blog/uk-skilled-worker-visa-resume-guide
- GOV.UK sponsorship overview (employer side): https://www.gov.uk/uk-visa-sponsorship-employers
- Lewis Silkin (law firm) on employers excluding candidates needing sponsorship — sponsorship is a legitimate knockout question in UK recruitment; many ATSs ask it explicitly.
  https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2025/05/08/the-employment-immigration-intersection-excluding-limiting-applications-candidates-need-sponsorship
- Synthesis: if candidate HAS unrestricted right to work but a non-UK background/qualifications, a one-line "Right to work in the UK (no sponsorship required)" pre-empts wrong assumptions. If sponsorship needed, don't hide it; state it when the JD asks or when applying to licensed sponsors.

## NHS / public sector
- NHS applications: usually via NHS Jobs/Trac application form, not CV. "Supporting information" section = evidence against every essential/desirable criterion in the person specification. Do NOT paste CV; map examples to criteria with outcomes.
  https://www.imperial.nhs.uk/~/media/website/careers/additional-guidance-notes-for-job-applicants.pdf
  https://passmrcpsych.com/how-to-write-supporting-information-for-nhs-jobs/
  https://www.jobs.nhs.uk/advice/success_applics.html
- Public sector generally (civil service): behaviours/success profiles, structured forms; CV often secondary.

## Finance / consulting
- IB CVs UK: strict ONE page for students/juniors; quantified impact (deal values, %, £); brand-name employers/universities weigh heavily; tailor per bank; ATS-clean.
  https://cvanywhere.com/blog/investment-banking-cv
  https://igotanoffer.com/blogs/finance/investment-banking-resume-examples
  https://uk.indeed.com/career-advice/cvs-cover-letters/investment-banking-cv
- Consulting: 1–2 pages accepted but extreme concision expected; impact-per-line scrutiny (PrepLounge).
  https://www.preplounge.com/en/blog/finance/investment-banking/interview/resume

## Tech / startups
- 87% of tech recruiters review GitHub profiles (Tribune/secondary stat — treat as directional); portfolio/GitHub link expected on CV; projects section critical for early-career; quality (3 polished, documented repos) over quantity; live demos/READMEs matter.
  https://tribuneonlineng.com/github-portfolio-masterclass-open-source-uk-tech-recruiters/
  https://machinelearningjobs.co.uk/career-advice/portfolio-projects-that-get-you-hired-for-machine-learning-jobs-with-real-github-examples-

## Academic CVs
- jobs.ac.uk: 3–5 pages typical (Oxford: no hard limit). Sections: contacts, qualifications, teaching, publications, grants/prizes, professional activities (refereeing, editorial, admin), referees (two). Reorder by job focus (teaching-led vs research-led). Most recent/most prestigious first per category. Simple 12pt font, bullets.
  https://career-advice.jobs.ac.uk/cv-and-cover-letter-advice/how-to-layout-an-academic-cv/
- Prospects academic CV example: https://www.prospects.ac.uk/careers-advice/cvs-and-cover-letters/example-cvs/academic-cv-example/

## International qualifications / UK ENIC
- UK ENIC (managed by Ecctis) issues Statement of Comparability: compares overseas qualification LEVEL to UK framework (e.g., "comparable to a British Bachelor (Honours) degree standard"). It does NOT compare grades — so "2:1 equivalent" claims must be self-assessed/justified, not ENIC-certified.
  https://www.enic.org.uk/individuals/statement-of-comparability
  https://www.enic.org.uk/
- Practice: on CV, give degree title + institution + country + years, then a parenthetical UK framing, e.g. "(equivalent to UK BSc (Hons); UK ENIC statement available)". Iranian grading: 20-point scale; ~17+/20 often treated as First-equivalent, ~15–17 as 2:1-band by UK universities' admissions pages — varies by institution, so prefer "GPA 17.2/20 (top 5% of cohort)" style evidence over asserting a classification.
- Degree classifications UK: First (1st), Upper second (2:1), Lower second (2:2), Third. Many grad schemes historically screened at 2:1; increasingly dropped but still common shorthand.

## Misc style
- UK spelling: organised, analyse, programme (of work; "program" for software), centre, labour, licence (noun)/license (verb), optimised, behaviour.
- Dates: "Sept 2024 – Present", "September 2024"; never US MM/DD.
- "CV" not "résumé" in all UK-facing materials.
